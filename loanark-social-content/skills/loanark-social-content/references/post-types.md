# Post types: shape, rules, and a worked example each

Every example below is written for a fictional agent, "Sam Rivera, Rivera Homes, DRE 01234567", working with a fictional loan officer "Jordan Lee, Loan Ark Inc., NMLS [LO NMLS]". Replace with the brand profile. Never ship these names.

## Educational carousel

Shape: slide 1 is the promise ("What 'pre-approved' actually means"), slides 2 to 6 one idea each in under 20 words, last slide the call to action and the co-brand block. Caption of 80 to 150 words restating the idea and asking one question.

Rules: facts from `program-facts.md` only; no numbers; define every term; "I" is the agent.

Example (slides):
1. Pre-qualified vs. pre-approved: they are not the same thing.
2. Pre-qualified: a conversation and an estimate. Nothing verified.
3. Pre-approved: documents reviewed, credit pulled, a lender has said "yes, subject to the house."
4. Listing agents read the letter next to your offer. Verified beats estimated.
5. The letter can be tailored to the home you are writing on, so it never overshoots.
6. Often done within a day with the right lender. Start before you fall in love with a house.
7. Text me and I'll connect you with Jordan at Loan Ark to get it done. [co-brand block]

## Myth vs. fact

Shape: three pairs, each "Myth: … / Fact: …" in two lines, then a one-line close and the short Loan Ark line.

Rules: the fact must be structural ("VA loans can be assumed by a qualified buyer") and never numeric; no "you can buy with as little as X% down".

Example: Myth: You need a huge down payment to buy. Fact: several programs are built for buyers with smaller savings, and gifts and assistance programs can help eligible buyers. Ask which ones fit you.

## Reel script (15 seconds)

Shape: hook (0 to 2 seconds, one line on screen), three beats (3 to 12 seconds, one line each), close (13 to 15 seconds, the ask). Then the caption and the on-screen text list.

Rules: no numbers; the agent on camera talks to one person; the close is the agent's call to action; the caption carries the short Loan Ark line if financing is mentioned.

Example: Hook: "Your offer got accepted. Now what?" Beat 1: "Sign your loan disclosures the day they arrive; the appraisal waits on them." Beat 2: "Get an insurance quote this week, not next month." Beat 3: "Don't open new credit or move money around. Ask first." Close: "Follow for what happens next, or text me your question."

## Market note (no-number version)

Shape: what is happening this week in one sentence, what it means for a buyer in one, for a seller in one, and the ask. If the Market Pulse figure and attribution are supplied, one attributed figure is allowed.

Rules: no predictions; no Loan Ark rate; the index is "a national average, not a quote".

## Rate-index alert

Shape: one line, attributed, no math. "30-year national average [X.XX]% today, [lowest/highest] since [date] (Optimal Blue Mortgage Market Indices via FRED, as of [date]). If you paused your search, it's worth a fresh look. —Sam"

Rules: only with a supplied or fetched figure and date; never a payment; never attached to Loan Ark, the LO, or the reader; the short Loan Ark line still applies because financing is the subject.

## Listing / open house / coming soon

Shape: fact first (the thing a buyer would text a friend about), three feature lines, date and time if open house, the financing line ("Curious what this would look like monthly? Jordan at Loan Ark can run it for you."), the ask, the co-brand block.

Rules: Fair Housing (describe the home, not the people), no invented facts, MLS facts confirmed in the before-you-post note; use the Loan Ark Listing Marketing skill for the full kit.

## Personal-brand post

Shape: a moment, a lesson, a line about why the agent does the work, an invitation to reply. No financing mention means no Loan Ark line; the agent's license identity stays.

Rules: nothing that identifies a client or a property in a story; no claims (awards, counts) that are not in the profile's "proof" section.

## Just-closed / client story

Shape: the celebration, one line about what the client overcame (generic), thanks to the team including "Jordan at Loan Ark", the co-brand block.

Rules: written permission on file; no address, price, loan details, or photo without consent; the buyer's side only names the address if the agent confirms it is public and they represented a side.

## Seasonal / homeowner tip

Shape: one useful tip for the season (fire season prep, freeze protection, insurance renewal timing, property-tax deadlines as "check your county's date"), the ask.

Rules: no legal or tax advice; "ask your tax advisor"; no numbers that go stale.

## Program explainer post

Shape: the program in one sentence, who it is generally for (as a need, not a demographic), the one thing people get wrong, "ask Jordan whether it fits", the co-brand block.

Rules: facts from `program-facts.md`; never "you qualify"; never a percentage or payment.

## Hashtags

3 to 5, one with the city ("#CostaMesaHomes"), the rest about the topic ("#FirstTimeBuyer", "#VALoan"). Never hashtags that name a group of people.
