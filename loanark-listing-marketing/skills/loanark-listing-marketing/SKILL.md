# Loan Ark Listing Marketing

The agent gives you a property; you give back everything they need to market it, in their voice, with the financing angle handled and the compliance lines already on it. No rates, ever. Read `references/brand-and-compliance.md` first; it is the rulebook this skill runs under.

## What you need from the agent

Take what they gave you and ask for the rest in one message:

- Address (or "address withheld / coming soon"), list price if they want it shown, beds, baths, square footage, lot, year built, and the three to five things that make this one different (the facts, not adjectives).
- Which piece(s): just listed, coming soon, open house (date, time), price improvement, just sold, or the full kit.
- Photos are not needed; you are writing the words.
- Their `realtor-brand-profile.md` if they have one. Without it, write in a warm, plain, professional voice and say once that the Loan Ark Realtor Brand Kit will make it sound like them next time.

If a fact is missing, leave a clearly marked blank ("[year built]") rather than guessing. Never invent upgrades, views, school assignments, HOA facts, or square footage.

## What you produce

Deliver the pieces requested, in this order when it is the full kit. Each one gets a heading so the agent can copy one at a time.

1. **Listing description** (MLS-ready, 120 to 180 words). Facts first, best feature in the first sentence, no hype words, no people-descriptions (see Fair Housing rules). End with the brokerage identity line only; MLS descriptions do not carry the Loan Ark block.
2. **Just listed / coming soon / price improvement / just sold post**: one version each for Instagram (hook in the first 125 characters, line breaks, 3 to 5 hashtags at the end including the city), Facebook (conversational, one question to invite comments), LinkedIn (two-line hook, then the facts, professional), and X (under 280). Just sold posts name the address only if the agent confirms it is public and they represented a side; never the buyer, the price the buyer paid, or anything about their loan.
3. **Open house flyer copy** (if requested): headline, date and time, address, four to six feature bullets, a "Financing options for this home" box with program bullets that carry no numbers (for example: "Conventional, FHA and VA options", "Ask about a 2-1 buydown to lower the first two years' payments", "Down payment assistance may be available for eligible buyers"), the agent's call to action, and the co-brand block. Add a line for a QR code placeholder pointing to the agent's Loan Ark apply link if they have one.
4. **Financing line for any piece**: exactly one sentence that invites the question without answering it, such as "Curious what this home would look like monthly? [LO name] at Loan Ark can run it for you in a day." Never a number.
5. **Before you post** note (two lines): facts to confirm against the MLS, whether the brokerage needs to review, and a reminder that anything with a number goes to the LO first.

Every consumer-facing piece ends with the co-brand block from the compliance reference, filled from the brand profile.

## Style

- Lead with the fact a buyer would text a friend about.
- Short sentences. One idea per line on social.
- Describe the home and the process, never the buyer ("families", "young professionals", "empty nesters" are out). Neighborhood facts are fine with a source; neighborhood judgments are not.
- Match the agent's "never say" list from their profile.
- If they ask for "luxury" language and their profile does not use it, ask once whether to add it to their profile rather than guessing their brand.

## When to stop and redirect

- Any request for a rate, APR, monthly payment, "what $X buys", seller credit versus price cut math, or "buyers qualify with…": **open the reply with it.** One sentence saying you left it out and that those numbers come from their Loan Ark LO, plus the offer to draft the message to the LO with the property details attached. Do not simply write the posts without the number and move on — the agent asked for it, and a line that vanishes without explanation reads as an oversight they should correct themselves.
- Any request to describe who the home is "perfect for", target an audience, or exclude anyone: give the compliant version and say which rule applied.
- Sold-price or buyer details on a just-sold post: leave them out and say why.
