# Loan Ark Buyer Explainers

The agent looks like the person who explains things clearly. That is the whole job. Every handout is written to the buyer or seller, signed by the agent, with Loan Ark as the financing partner in the footer. Read `references/brand-and-compliance.md` first.

Pick the closest one; combine two if the client's situation calls for it.

## How to write one

1. Ask only what changes the piece: the client's situation in one line (if any), the agent's brand profile (if not attached), and which state the client is buying in (CA, CO, TX). Do not ask for anything else.
2. Open with the one thing the reader most wants to know, in the agent's voice. No preamble, no "in today's market".
3. Explain in steps or short sections with plain headings. Define every term the first time. Use "you" for the reader and "I" for the agent.
4. Financing facts are structural only: how a program works, who it is generally for, what documents come up, what the order of operations is. **No rates, APRs, payments, dollar examples, down payment amounts or percentages, or "you'd qualify."** Where a number is what the reader will want next, write: "[LO name] at Loan Ark can run your exact numbers, often within a day."
   - This holds even when the agent names a specific property and a specific client, and even when the arithmetic is public and checkable. Loan limits, LTV caps and program ceilings are facts you may *name* ("this price is under the conforming limit for the county, so it is not a jumbo") — applying them to produce a figure the buyer would need at closing is the LO's job, because only the LO knows which programs this borrower can actually use.
   - A handout is reusable by nature; that is what makes it a handout. Do not treat "this is for one client I'm meeting Thursday" as a reason the rule does not apply, and do not write the figure with a note about when it would become a problem.
5. Close with the agent's call to action from their profile, then the co-brand block.
6. Add the two-line "before you send" note: what the agent should personalize, and that the piece carries no numbers on purpose.

## Formats

Default is a clean one-page handout (markdown the agent can paste into their email or a doc). On request, also produce: a 5-slide carousel outline (one idea per slide, under 20 words each), a short email version (120 to 180 words), or a text-message version (under 320 characters) that links to the handout.

## Guardrails that come up here

- Never say a program is available to a specific client; say who it is "generally for" and send them to the LO.
- Down payment assistance, grants, and buydowns: describe the mechanism, never a dollar amount, and never promise availability.
- No comparisons that need a number to be honest (for example "a buydown saves you $X"). Explain the shape of the tradeoff and hand the math to the LO.
- Keep tax and legal points to "ask your tax advisor" and "ask your attorney"; the agent is not licensed to give either and neither is Loan Ark.
- Everything in the Fair Housing section of the compliance reference applies, including in "who this is for" lines.
